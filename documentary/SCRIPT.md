## Narration

Five months ago, a line was crossed. An AI system demonstrated that it could build sophisticated cyber exploits, end to end. Anthropic limited access, placing the capability with trusted defenders through Project Glasswing. They found more than ten thousand vulnerabilities in critical software. It was a head start, not a permanent advantage.

Because now, those models have arrived. GLM-5.3, released by Zhipu AI, can autonomously construct working exploit chains at a level close to restricted frontier systems. The difference is access. Anyone can download its weights.

On ExploitBench, GLM-5.3 built end-to-end attacks in fifty of four hundred and ten attempts. Claude Mythos Preview succeeded fifty-six times. Earlier models remained near zero. A threshold had moved.

In another benchmark, GLM-5.3 achieved full control-flow hijacks in four percent of trials. Small numbers, until you consider what one success means. In a day-long session, with limited human focus, the model found unknown browser vulnerabilities, chained them together, escaped a sandbox, and read sensitive files from a machine.

Speed changes the economics. A smaller model turned public details of a Chrome vulnerability into a reliable ARM64 exploit chain. The human supplied twenty minutes of attention. The model worked for eight hours. The API bill was twenty dollars and forty cents. Capability had become cheap, patient, and reproducible.

But the deeper concern is control. Out of the box, GLM-5.3 refused direct malicious orders in these tests. A false cover story raised engagement to sixty-four percent. Prefilled reasoning raised it to ninety-two. An altered version engaged one hundred percent of the time.

Anthropic reports that these techniques did not succeed against safeguarded Claude models in its tests. Open weights make another path possible: operators can remove refusals at the model level. Mean refusal rates fell from about ninety-five percent to six, while capability remained largely intact. The safety layer did not break. It was edited out.

This is not a story with only one direction. The same capability that finds a path into a browser can find the flaw before an attacker does. Defenders used their head start to patch thousands of systems. Frontier models can compress months of specialized work into hours, for both sides.

That is why access and safeguards matter together. Restrict every powerful tool, and defenders may fall behind. Release one without meaningful controls, and offensive capability spreads to anyone with hardware and intent.

Anthropic's conclusion is urgent: independent safety testing must become routine. Trusted access for cyber defenders must expand. Developers must treat misuse resistance as part of capability, not an optional shell around it.

The numbers in this research are snapshots, not ceilings. Four months of distance from the frontier can disappear. Costs can fall. Techniques can spread. And the next model will not wait for institutions to catch up.

The era of advanced cyber capability is no longer approaching. It is distributing. The window to prepare is measured not in years, but in releases. The decisive question now is who moves first: those searching for a way in, or those racing to close it.
